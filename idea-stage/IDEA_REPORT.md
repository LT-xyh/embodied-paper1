# Third Embodied AI / VLA Research-Direction Discovery — 2026-09-27

## Scope and governance

This is a new direction-discovery cycle. The frozen negative evidence for Action-Swap Consequence Consistency (ASCC) and Causal Action-Effect Alignment (CAEA) is treated as a hard boundary. This report does not rescue, rename, scale, or retune either candidate. ASCC failed its scientific pilot: its recurrent auxiliary signal was near chance and indistinguishable from shuffled controls. CAEA failed the policy-candidate-score identifiability gate: ordinary robomimic `BC_RNN` exposes a deterministic tanh action and no native score over arbitrary continuous candidates. The native MuJoCo intervention substrate and robosuite wrapper state-closure qualification remain reusable measurement instruments only.

The new search therefore uses directly observable quantities: simulator state, policy observation/history, the deterministic action actually selected, executable alternatives, real successor states and observations, reward/done/task quantities, and (when technically available) hidden state as an observed diagnostic. No candidate assumes `pi(a|h)`, candidate log-probability, native preference, or confidence over alternatives. No method implementation, training, runtime repair, VLA inference, simulator rollout, or scientific pilot was run here.

The target is a focused, publishable empirical/diagnostic result that can be killed in a 1–3 day CPU pilot and can be evaluated on more than one manipulation task. A candidate is admitted only if its scientific object is identifiable before implementation and its distinction from robustness, perturbation testing, memory auditing, action-space design, and generic policy evaluation is explicit.

## Literature landscape

Recent work makes a generic “counterfactual policy evaluation” claim unsafe. The Counterfactual Memory Audit (CMA, arXiv:2609.27247, September 2026) crosses two lawful histories at a verified-identical present, queries a frozen policy, and evaluates saved actions in both matched worlds. It already separates memory sensitivity from warranted choice, matched-world physical value, and reliability. Any proposal whose main result is simply that an intervention changes a history-conditioned action is rejected as an ASCC/CMA-neighborhood idea. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27247.

RoboART (Predictive Red Teaming, arXiv:2502.06575 / ICLR 2026) predicts manipulation-policy failures under edited environmental factors without hardware trials, using policy-specific anomaly rates. RoboMD (ICLR 2026) learns a separate vulnerability-search policy in a semantic potential field and reports a vulnerability-likelihood map. These works make a generic “find perturbations that break a robot policy” story non-novel. Neither, however, executes a balanced neighborhood of valid continuous actions from the *same physical state* and studies how the selected action’s real temporal consequence geometry explains failure. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06575 and https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/b364c8cbb3f229f40d5873e877391bd2-Abstract-Conference.html.

Action-consequence conditioning is also now an active VLA direction. Reflective VLA (arXiv:2606.25215) conditions decisions on observation-action-consequence triplets and shows cross-environment gains; it studies learning a more adaptive policy, not a frozen-policy intervention audit. A proposal that adds consequence history, an auxiliary prediction loss, or a memory module is therefore rejected as an ASCC-like method variant. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25215.

Temporal credit and long-horizon consequence estimation are crowded. PACE (arXiv:2608.15026) learns a phase-progress-aware critic and distills credit into a policy; “When Should a Robot Replan?” (arXiv:2606.16972) derives regret-guided update scheduling in drifting MDPs. These works motivate checking multi-horizon consequences, but they do not establish a fixed-state, real-transition diagnostic of whether the action selected by a deterministic imitation policy is immediately helpful yet temporally harmful. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15026 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16972.

Action-space and robustness literature further narrows the claim. “Demystifying Action Space Design for Robotic Manipulation” (arXiv:2602.23408) studies absolute/delta and joint/task-space parameterizations with thousands of real rollouts; Action-Constrained Imitation Learning (ICML 2025) imposes action constraints; SIMPLER, Trustworthy Evaluation of Robotic Manipulation, and ManipEvalAgent provide broad evaluation protocols. A new action perturbation score, action-equivalence clustering, or action-space substitution would be a metric or benchmark change unless it yields a distinct causal mechanism. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23408, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/yeh25a.html, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05941, and https://openreview.net/pdf?id=3u6AkbWEls.

The resulting opening is narrow: a frozen deterministic policy can be audited at an exactly cloned state with real simulator transitions, while treating its selected action as an observed decision rather than as a probability or preference. The promising question is whether a selected action can be locally helpful at one temporal scale but causally harmful at a longer scale. A second basin-geometry question was screened as a comparator but is not retained after the independent novelty audit because it collapses toward local robustness and action-space geometry. The surviving question is an intervention-based decomposition of observed behavior; it proposes neither a recurrent loss nor a learned score or world-model evaluator.

## Ranked ideas

The initial pool contains five genuinely different measurement directions; aggressive prior and identifiability screening retains only TCI as a cautious survivor.

### Initial candidate pool and aggressive filters

Five directions were considered before screening:

1. **Temporal Consequence Inversion (TCI)** — fixed-state, real-transition test for immediate-versus-long-horizon ordering reversals around the action actually selected by a deterministic policy.
2. **Local Consequence Basin Geometry (LCBG)** — fixed-state, balanced executable neighborhood that measures width, anisotropy, and boundary margin of the local task-success basin.
3. **Chosen-Action Responsibility Decomposition (CARD)** — classify a failure as action-caused when a nearby alternative recovers and state-limited when all alternatives fail.
4. **Controller–Policy Consequence Distortion (CPCD)** — measure whether controller saturation, dead zones, or action-map amplification reshape policy-space perturbations into different physical outcomes.
5. **Memory/observation intervention diagnostics** — swap histories or observations and score action changes or successor changes.

CARD was rejected because the action-caused/state-limited partition is already the natural counterfactual-failure and vulnerability-discovery claim of RoboMD, PACT/MILE-style intervention learning, and CMA-like matched-world audits. CPCD was rejected because it is an action-space/controller characterization close to action-space design and engineering validation, with no distinct policy-learning mechanism. Memory/observation interventions were rejected because CMA, Reflective VLA, ASCC, and the accumulated visual-shortcut evidence already cover the relevant causal question. TCI survives only as a cautious diagnostic, subject to the independent review and the exact closest-prior table below. LCBG is carried through the table only to make its rejection auditable.

### 1. Temporal Consequence Inversion (TCI) — provisional rank 1

**Failure mode.** A deterministic imitation policy can choose an action that improves an immediate task-progress proxy but moves the system into a lower-value contact or branching state at a longer horizon. Episode success alone does not identify this myopic failure mode.

**Scientific hypothesis.** At a verified cloned state, a nontrivial fraction of impending failures contain a *temporal consequence inversion*: for the selected action `a_hat` and a matched executable alternative `a_i`, the sign of their task-progress difference at horizon `H_short` reverses at `H_long`. The prevalence and lead time of these inversions should predict failure beyond action magnitude, one-step progress, and state distance to demonstrations. The hypothesis concerns observed selected actions and real successor outcomes; it makes no claim that the policy preferred `a_i`.

**Exact measurable object.** Let `G_H(s_t,a)` be a preregistered task-progress functional computed from the real rollout branch starting from cloned wrapper state `s_t`, using horizon `H` and common environment random seeds. For each alternative `a_i`, define `D_H(i)=G_H(s_t,a_hat)-G_H(s_t,a_i)`. A pair is an inversion when `sign(D_Hshort(i)) != sign(D_Hlong(i))` and the absolute differences exceed a fixed task-scale margin. Report inversion prevalence, the selected-action long-horizon regret relative to the best tested alternative, and the earliest inversion-to-failure lead time. `G_H` uses simulator/task quantities (object-to-goal progress, stage completion, contact validity, reward/done) and is therefore directly observable.

**Closest-prior table.**

| Exact prior claim | What it already solves | Non-overlapping TCI claim | Distinguishing experiment | Mechanism or metric change? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PACE, arXiv:2608.15026, learns a phase-progress-aware critic and policy distillation for long-horizon VLA post-training. | It supplies learned step-level credit and improves long-horizon manipulation. | TCI does not learn a critic or modify a policy; it tests whether a frozen deterministic BC action is locally helpful but temporally harmful under real same-state branches. | Compare real branch-level inversion labels against PACE-style phase/credit and one-step progress on Lift/Can, with no learned world model. | A scientific decomposition if inversion predicts failures after controls; otherwise only a metric change. |
| When Should a Robot Replan?, arXiv:2606.16972, derives regret-guided update timing for drifting MDPs. | It allocates replanning under transition drift and compute budgets. | TCI studies contact-rich imitation-policy decisions in stationary simulator tasks and asks whether temporal ordering reverses before failure, without scheduling replans. | Hold policy and environment fixed; test inversion prevalence and failure lead time before any replanning rule. | Mechanism (temporal ordering inversion) only if it survives horizon and progress controls. |
| PACT/MILE-style counterfactual intervention learning (PACT arXiv:2606.03949; MILE ICRA 2025). | They use intervention/counterfactual signals to improve human-in-the-loop correction or intervention modeling. | TCI is a frozen-policy audit of real successor consequences, with no human intervention target or learned correction policy. | Use the same cloned states but compare diagnostic inversion to intervention labels and random alternatives. | Mechanism if it isolates myopia; otherwise generic counterfactual evaluation. |
| RoboMD, ICLR 2026. | It searches semantic/environment vulnerability regions with a separate RL policy and predicts vulnerability likelihood. | TCI does not search environment appearance or train a vulnerability policy; it attributes an observed failure to a temporal ordering reversal among executable actions at the same state. | Equal-state action branches with common wrapper closure; compare inversion to RoboMD-style vulnerability score where available. | Mechanism if action ordering adds predictive information; otherwise a narrower metric. |

**Cheapest discriminating pilot (not run).** Use state-only robomimic `BC_RNN` on Lift and Can. Select 30–50 held-out states per task from successful and failing policy rollouts, with at least 20% contact/branching states. Restore each state in independent subprocesses. Execute `a_hat` and 4–8 bounded alternatives matched by action norm and validity. Use `H_short={1,2}` and `H_long={8,16}` (task control rate fixed), common random seeds, and a preregistered margin. Report paired inversion prevalence, selected-action long-horizon regret, and failure lead time. Controls are one-step progress only, action norm, nearest-demonstration distance, random alternative rank, and a next-state-prediction ranking if a lightweight offline predictor is available.

**Identifiability and outcomes.** Every variable is an observed action, cloned state, or real successor task quantity. No candidate score or counterfactual policy likelihood is needed. Positive evidence requires the same-sign pre-failure effect on Lift and Can, a bootstrap confidence interval for inversion prevalence difference excluding zero, and an improvement of at least 0.10 in failure AUC over the strongest non-interventional control. Null evidence means inversions occur but add no prediction beyond one-step progress or action distance; the claim is reduced to descriptive analysis. Negative evidence is task-specific, horizon/tolerance-sensitive, or absent before failure; abandon TCI.

**Main confound and effort.** Sparse or poorly chosen progress functionals can manufacture reversals, and alternatives may be infeasible or too far from the selected action. Equal-norm feasibility filters, task-specific preregistration, multiple fixed horizons, and common random seeds are mandatory. Instrumentation is moderate (1–2 days CPU; 2 tasks, 3 policy seeds, roughly 300–600 branch rollouts); no VLA training or large downloads.

### 2. Local Consequence Basin Geometry (LCBG) — rejected by novelty review

**Failure mode.** Contact dynamics can make the physically successful successor set around a deterministic action narrow and directionally asymmetric. A policy may fail because its selected command lies on a basin boundary even when its observation/action error is small.

**Scientific hypothesis.** Around a cloned state, the fraction and directional geometry of nearby executable actions that preserve short-horizon task progress form a local consequence basin. Basin width, anisotropy, and selected-action boundary margin predict failure onset across tasks better than Euclidean action noise or observation distance. The claim is about real successor geometry induced by contact and task state, not robustness to arbitrary image/environment perturbations.

**Exact measurable object.** Construct a balanced set `A_t={a_hat + δ_j}` with fixed norm shells, validity checks, and symmetric directions in the controller action coordinates. Execute each action for a fixed horizon `H` from identical cloned states. Define `B_t` as the fraction whose successor satisfies a preregistered progress/contact criterion; estimate directional anisotropy from the variance of successor progress over directions; define boundary margin as the minimum shell radius at which the selected action’s neighborhood crosses from progress-preserving to failure. Report these quantities with bootstrap intervals and task/contact strata.

**Closest-prior table.**

| Exact prior claim | What it already solves | Non-overlapping LCBG claim | Distinguishing experiment | Mechanism or metric change? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RoboART, arXiv:2502.06575 / ICLR 2026, predicts failures under edited environmental factors using anomaly rates. | It identifies visual/environment factors that degrade policy success without hardware trials. | LCBG holds the physical state fixed and varies only valid executable actions; it measures contact-local successor basin geometry, not observation/environment-factor robustness. | At matched states, compare action-shell basin features with RoboART-style observation anomaly and action distance. | Mechanism only if basin features add failure prediction after those controls. |
| RoboMD, ICLR 2026, searches semantic potential fields for vulnerability regions. | It discovers unknown vulnerability variations and maps failure likelihood. | LCBG is an exhaustive, small, same-state action neighborhood and does not train a search policy or produce a semantic vulnerability map. | Use balanced action shells from identical states and compare basin metrics with vulnerability-map scores. | Often a metric; admitted only if contact-conditioned geometry explains failures. |
| Demystifying Action Space Design, arXiv:2602.23408. | It measures how action parameterization (absolute/delta, joint/task space) changes imitation learning across thousands of rollouts. | LCBG audits a frozen policy under its existing controller and asks whether local physical basin shape predicts its failures; it does not compare action representations or retrain policies. | Keep controller/action parameterization fixed; stratify basin metrics by contact phase and policy success. | Mechanism if policy failure is explained by basin boundary position, otherwise action-space robustness. |
| Action-Constrained Imitation Learning, ICML 2025. | It constrains training actions and improves trajectory alignment. | LCBG does not impose constraints or alter training; it measures the realized feasible successor set after the policy acts. | Compare basin metrics with constrained-vs-unconstrained policies as a follow-up only if the diagnostic passes. | Mechanism only after a preregistered causal relationship; otherwise metric change. |

**Cheapest discriminating pilot (not run).** The proposed 30–50-state, 12-action-shell test was retained only as a control design for TCI. It is not an independent P0 because the novelty review found that the resulting basin fraction, anisotropy, and boundary margin reduce to local robustness/action-space geometry.

**Identifiability and outcomes.** State restoration, action commands, and successor features are directly observable, but identifiability alone is insufficient: the independent audit found no non-overlapping mechanism after controlling for action norm, contact, and shell choices. LCBG is therefore rejected for Paper-1 and may appear only as a TCI control variable.

**Main confound and effort.** The basin depends on coordinate choice, shell radius, contact discontinuities, and a hand-designed progress threshold. These confounds are the reason it is not retained as a standalone direction.

### Rejected candidates and hard negative boundaries

- **CARD / causal responsibility decomposition:** rejected as a direct counterfactual failure-attribution and vulnerability-search restatement of RoboMD, PACT/MILE, and CMA. A recovery alternative can be retained only as a control variable inside TCI/LCBG, not as the contribution.
- **CPCD / controller–policy distortion:** rejected as controller engineering and action-space characterization, too close to action-space design and redundancy studies.
- **ASCC-neighborhood variants:** any recurrent hidden-state auxiliary loss, consequence prediction loss, transition-surprise memory, action-swap consistency, or renamed predictive regularizer is rejected by the ASCC failure and CMA/Reflective-VLA proximity.
- **CAEA-style candidate scores:** rejected before implementation because deterministic `BC_RNN` has no native candidate-action likelihood or preference; post-hoc action distance is not a score.
- **Generic robustness, visual shortcut, uncertainty, safety, world-model action-following, benchmark-only, and action-entropy ideas:** rejected by accumulated negative evidence and RoboART/RoboMD/WorldEcho/SIMPLER-style priors.

## External critical review

**Independent novelty review receipt.** `gpt-6-astra / medium`, thread `/root/third_direction_novelty_review`. The reviewer read this report and the prior ASCC/CAEA evidence without implementation or pilot execution. Verdict: **TCI — PROCEED WITH CAUTION**; **LCBG — ABANDON**. TCI is defensible only as a fixed-state, real-transition temporal sign-reversal diagnostic. Its strongest overlap is PACE/finite-horizon credit and PACT/MILE counterfactual intervention analysis. The reviewer required held-out predictive gain beyond one-step progress, action norm, contact, and nearest-demonstration controls; common-seed cloned-state branches; and a predeclared horizon/margin. The reviewer also required that finite-alternative regret be reported as a sampled lower bound, never as global regret or policy preference, and warned against selecting “impending failures” after observing outcomes. LCBG was rejected because same-state action shells, basin width, anisotropy, and boundary margin collapse toward RoboART/RoboMD vulnerability testing and action-space geometry. CARD, CPCD, ASCC-neighborhood variants, and CAEA score substitutes remain rejected.

**Final critical-review receipt.** `gpt-6-astra / medium`, thread `/root/third_direction_critical_review`, recorded in `idea-stage/TCI_FINAL_CRITICAL_REVIEW_RECEIPT.md`. Verdict: **TCI — PROCEED WITH CAUTION**; LCBG remains **ABANDON**. The reviewer confirms that TCI is identifiable only as the observed deterministic selected action plus real same-state branch sign reversal; it must not imply likelihood, preference, confidence, global regret, or causal regret over the whole action space. Finite alternatives are a sampled lower bound. The strongest objection is finite-horizon credit/robustness relabeling, especially overlap with PACE. Survival requires pre-outcome state selection, complete fresh-process wrapper/RNG closure, equal-radius valid alternatives, common seeds, two fixed horizon pairs, frozen progress/margins, episode-level held-out splits, and incremental held-out failure prediction over one-step progress, action norm, contact, nearest-demo, random ranking, and optional next-state controls on both Lift and Can. The receipt specifies positive, null, and hard-kill outcomes. No candidate is implementation-authorized by this report.

## Refinement and experiment planning boundary

The current recommendation is **at most one immediate preflight: TCI**, contingent on a positive final review and a clean runtime. The preflight is an offline protocol audit plus a tiny 6-state-per-task smoke test only after explicit future authorization; this discovery cycle does not run it. LCBG is a rejected comparator, not a backup direction. A pilot is killed if same-state null replay fails, if action alternatives are not valid or norm-matched, if TCI adds no held-out predictive information over non-interventional controls, if state selection leaks the future failure label, or if the signal is task/horizon/tolerance-specific.

No `BC_RNN` training, simulator rollout, VLA inference, runtime installation, data/checkpoint download, GPU/DCU job, or real-robot experiment was performed in this cycle.
