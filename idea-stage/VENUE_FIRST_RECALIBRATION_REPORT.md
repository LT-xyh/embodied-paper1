## 3. Observed Contribution Patterns

The sample shows a two-level bar rather than a single “journal novelty” threshold.

* **New mechanisms remain common** in RA-L, IJRR, T-RO, and RAS when the mechanism changes the control or learning problem and is tested beyond one toy task. These papers often include real hardware, theory, or a strong sim-to-real story.
* **Principled combinations are clearly publishable in journals.** Adaptive wiping combines feedback and representation learning; PPF combines imitation, RL, and a state-dependent regularizer; Dynamic Adaptive DWA combines a learned component with a classical planner; RUMOR combines future-scene information with DRL. The combination is accepted because it addresses a concrete robot failure mode and changes closed-loop behavior.
* **Incremental method extensions can pass a journal bar** when the problem is concrete, the delta is technically meaningful, and the evaluation is broad. The few-shot manipulation study in RAS is an example of a smaller method/empirical delta than the flagship T-RO papers.
* **Empirical studies and benchmarks are accepted** when they introduce a useful benchmark, a repeatable comparison protocol, or a nontrivial empirical question. FTR-Bench is a benchmark contribution rather than a new learner; its value comes from the task design, open implementation, and multi-algorithm baselines.
* **System/application contributions are accepted** when the system has a technical mechanism and field evidence. JFR’s orchard manipulator and MultiPlan field study are not merely an application of an off-the-shelf model.
* **Reviews and position papers are accepted** in IJRR, RAS, and JFR when they provide a taxonomy, synthesis, or analysis that advances the field. They do not imply that a benchmark-only Paper-1 study will be accepted; they show that “new learning mechanism” is not a universal journal requirement.
* **The experiment package matters more as novelty is reduced.** The lower-bar papers still provide multiple tasks/conditions, meaningful baselines, ablations or comparisons, uncertainty/robustness analysis, and enough implementation/data detail for replication. A one-task one-seed action-MSE gain would not match the sample.
* **Real-robot evidence is venue-dependent.** JFR explicitly says simulation-only results are insufficient. RA-L and the flagship transactions sample frequently use real hardware, but their public scope does not make hardware mandatory for every paper. RAS and IJRR publish substantial simulation/theory/empirical work when the scientific contribution is clear.

The previous project gate treated “not a new mechanism” as almost equivalent to “not publishable.” The sample does not support that equivalence for a CAS Zone 3-or-above journal, while it remains a reasonable approximation for a CCF-B conference target.

## 4. Fast-Journal Contribution Bar (Track A)

**Track A minimum bar:** one technically meaningful, robot-specific incremental method or principled combination that changes a policy/control behavior, with a clear falsifiable problem statement and reproducible evidence. It must be stronger than a loss swap, modality substitution, optimizer change, dataset audit, or benchmark-only report.

For this project, an admissible Track-A paper should meet all of the following:

1. **One nontrivial delta:** a new control-aware objective, state-dependent combination, training/evaluation coupling, or lightweight adaptation that is not a direct prior collision. The mechanism can combine known parts if the robot-specific interaction between them is the scientific claim.
2. **Closed-loop evidence:** success/return, failure mode, or task completion is the primary metric. Action MSE or offline correlation can diagnose but cannot carry the claim.
3. **Breadth:** at least three primary robomimic task families or an equivalent multi-task set, fixed-seed repetitions, strong unmodified BC/BC-RNN baselines, ablations isolating the delta, and a concrete independent path such as Meta-World MT1/ML1. The second path should test the same mechanism without redesign.
4. **Robustness and reproducibility:** held-out initial states or task parameters, at least one controlled shift or generalization test, public configuration/code plan, and resource accounting compatible with the DCU/CPU constraints.
5. **Venue fit:** RA-L-first work should be concise and technically focused; a RAS/JFR full-length version may add analysis and field/analog evidence. A real robot is not mandatory for every Track-A target, but it is required for JFR and materially strengthens RA-L/RAS submissions.
6. **Timeline:** a first draft within 1–2 months is realistic only after the candidate clears a static direct-prior audit and the baseline path is healthy. The contribution should be implementable in a few focused days, leaving time for multi-task evaluation and writing.

Track A therefore permits a meaningful incremental extension or principled combination that the former conference gate would have rejected for insufficient conceptual novelty. It still rejects direct reproductions, the explicitly banned directions, benchmark QA without a policy consequence, and results that are only statistical associations.

## 5. CCF-B Conference Contribution Bar (Track B)

**Track B remains the former stronger bar:** a non-overlapping learning/control mechanism or theory claim that addresses a robotics-specific failure mode, survives an aggressive 2025–2026 prior audit, and produces a clear policy-level effect across multiple tasks and an independent benchmark.

An admissible Track-B paper should additionally provide:

* a mechanism that is more than a principled assembly of standard components unless the assembly exposes a new general principle;
* a strong comparison to the closest current method, not only to vanilla BC;
* broad ablations and stress/generalization tests that distinguish the mechanism from tuning or data quality;
* a credible route to real-robot or unusually strong sim-to-real evidence when the target conference expects it;
* a result that remains scientifically meaningful if robomimic is replaced by another benchmark.

The conference bar is not relaxed by the journal audit. CCF-B remains appropriate for genuinely new policy mechanisms, theoretical guarantees, or strong cross-embodiment/control claims. The project should not call a Track-A incremental result a Track-B mechanism contribution.

## 6. Previous Near-Miss Reassessment

### Direct collisions remain rejected

The following are not reopened under Track A: GPC-equivalent policy composition, Q-Planning-equivalent frozen-BC/Q selection, ISR-equivalent trajectory resampling, SDP-equivalent correction loss, MTIL/Mamba Policy-equivalent SSM policy design, conformal robot safety/ConformalDAgger-equivalent uncertainty, and distributionally robust offline imitation learning already covered by direct 2026 work. Lowering the venue bar cannot turn a direct prior collision into a distinct contribution.

### Near-misses rejected for weak scientific consequence

* **Armnet outcome-dependent trajectory-support analysis:** the venue sample confirms that benchmark/evaluation papers can be publishable, but the project’s P0.5 evidence showed only a small content-plus-length increment, weak/non-specific bimanual replication, and no robust policy-ranking consequence. Its rejection was caused by weak scientific consequence and lack of a causal policy claim, not only by a conference-level novelty bar. It remains out.
* **Adjacent-field C3 concept-drift robust BC:** this looked closest to a Track-A principled combination, but it is distributionally robust offline imitation learning and therefore falls under the explicit direct-prior exclusion. It cannot be revived by changing venues.
* **The other adjacent-field candidates:** Mamba/SSM, conformal action sets, test-time entropy adaptation, and trajectory-risk selection each collided with an explicit robotics prior or an accumulated negative family. They are not journal-bar near-misses.
* **Baseline-first C1–C5:** AEM, GPC, Q-Planning, ISR, and SDP provide direct mechanism collisions. The fact that a journal might publish a smaller extension does not make a modality/scale/preprocessing transfer a non-overlapping claim.

**Strongest previous near-miss that becomes viable under Track A: none.** The audit does show that a *new* incremental or combination method could be viable for a journal, but none of the recorded candidates has a remaining nontrivial delta after the mandatory exclusions. No prior candidate is authorized for a P0.

## 7. Recommended Publication Strategy

### Final strategy verdict

**FAST-JOURNAL BAR ADOPTED**

Adopt a **RA-L-first fast-journal strategy**. RA-L is a verified 2025 CAS Robotics 2区 venue with a concise format and a scope that explicitly accepts innovative robotics methods and application results. The target is a focused, reproducible robot-learning contribution at the Track-A bar, with the manuscript shaped to remain compatible with a full-length RAS submission only if its CAS classification is independently verified at submission time. IJRR and T-RO remain calibration upper bounds; JFR is unsuitable for the current simulation/offline-only core because its author guide requires field or appropriate analog validation.

This decision changes the admission bar, not the accumulated negative evidence. It allows the next search to consider a technically meaningful incremental method or principled combination that would not clear the former CCF-B mechanism bar, while preserving direct-prior, closed-loop, reproducibility, and multi-task gates.

## 8. Recommended Next Search Contract (do not execute here)

The next contract should be a fresh **RA-L-first Track-A Method Search**, separate from the completed discovery passes. It should require:

1. Start from one concrete robot-learning failure mode and generate at most three method hypotheses; do not produce a broad idea list.
2. Admit only a new incremental mechanism or principled combination with a named non-overlapping 2025–2026 prior delta. Reject GPC, Q-Planning, ISR, SDP, MTIL/Mamba Policy, conformal robot safety/ConformalDAgger, distributionally robust offline imitation, and every accumulated negative family before implementation.
3. Use the conditionally admitted robomimic + robosuite + MuJoCo state-only BC/BC-RNN path as the primary substrate. Plan a Meta-World MT1/ML1 or equivalent independent evaluation path before method code is written.
4. Define a 1–3 day closed-loop falsification pilot: exact baseline, tasks, primary success/return metric, seed plan, positive criterion, kill criterion, CPU/DCU budget, and no new runtime reconstruction.
5. If the pilot passes, expand to the Track-A package: at least three task families, multiple seeds, ablations, a held-out shift/generalization test, and reproducible code/configuration. Do not claim a CCF-B mechanism result unless the stronger Track-B bar is separately met.
6. If no candidate clears the direct-prior and closed-loop gates, record `NO CANDIDATE` again; do not lower the scientific claim to benchmark QA or a one-loss comparison merely to fit RA-L.
