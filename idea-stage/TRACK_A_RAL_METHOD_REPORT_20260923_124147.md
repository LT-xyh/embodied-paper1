## 2. Three-or-Fewer Candidate Hypotheses

Two hypotheses received serious screening; one survives the current admission gate.

### A. Transition-surprise-triggered hidden-state reset for BC-RNN — RETAIN WITH CAUTION

**Failure mode.** A recurrent policy’s hidden state summarizes the recent state-action path. A contact slip or externally caused object displacement can make that path inaccurate while the recurrent state continues to encode the pre-disturbance plan. The current state may reveal the new object pose, but the stale hidden state can still bias the next corrective action.

**Scientific hypothesis.** In state-only closed-loop imitation, an unusually large one-step physical transition residual identifies moments when the pre-event BC-RNN state is harmful; resetting that state before the next action improves post-disturbance task success over both no additional reset and a reset schedule with the same expected frequency.

**Method delta.** Fit a small one-step predictor of next object/end-effector state from the current state and commanded action using the demonstration training split. Normalize its prediction residual using a held-out nominal split. During rollout, reset the BC-RNN hidden state and cell state before computing the next action when the residual exceeds the fixed validation threshold. Keep the standard policy, training loss, action space, and robomimic horizon reset unchanged.

**Closest prior.** GMP is the closest 2026 policy-memory prior: it learns an observation-conditioned binary gate for whether cached history should be read, with gate labels calibrated from the action-prediction-error benefit of memory. It also evaluates a mid-task physical cup perturbation. The proposed trigger instead uses the mismatch between a predicted physical transition and its observed outcome, then destructively resets a recurrent policy state. [GMP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18933), [GMP method and evaluation](https://gated-memory-policy.github.io/).

**Non-overlapping Track-A claim.** A calibrated post-action state-transition innovation can identify when a recurrent BC policy’s pre-event history should be invalidated, improving immediate closed-loop recovery beyond reset-frequency-matched controls.

**Primary baseline and tasks.** robomimic v0.5.x state-only BC-RNN trained on public Lift and Can proficient-human demonstrations, using robosuite v1.5.x and MuJoCo. Keep each task’s data split, policy, seeds, and native horizon-reset configuration identical across inference variants.

**Implementation scope.** One small state-transition predictor, a held-out residual threshold, and an inference-time conditional call to the policy’s existing reset path. The robomimic BC-RNN implementation already maintains recurrent state, resets it at configured horizons, and exposes reset behavior; see the [v0.5.0 BC-RNN source](https://github.com/ARISE-Initiative/robomimic/blob/v0.5.0/robomimic/algo/bc.py) and [RNN policy network](https://github.com/ARISE-Initiative/robomimic/blob/v0.5.0/robomimic/models/policy_nets.py).

**Positive criterion.** Under the targeted perturbation, event-triggered reset improves absolute success by at least 10 percentage points over the same-policy/no-extra-reset baseline on both Lift and Can, and by at least 5 points over the rate-matched random-reset control. The effect direction must agree across all three seeds. Nominal success may not fall by more than 5 points; at least 80% of injected events must trigger within two control steps, while no more than 5% of nominal episodes may receive a false reset.

**Kill criterion.** Kill if closed-loop success does not improve, the gain appears only in action MSE, the effect occurs on only one task without a mechanistic explanation, the rate-matched reset achieves the same result, the nominal false-reset rate exceeds the bound, or a direct prior with the same transition-residual-triggered hard reset and recovery effect is found.

**Second evaluation path.** Meta-World ML1 with state-only scripted-expert trajectories for Pick-Place, Reach, and Drawer-Open. Generate the paired demonstrations from the benchmark’s included policies only after the P0 admission step; do not use Meta-World during this selection pass.

**Qualitative assessment.** Scientific clarity: high. Track-A novelty safety: moderate-to-low; the delta is specific but close to 2026 memory gating and recurrent-reset work. P0 speed: 1–3 days if the admitted stack is already usable. Runtime risk: low-to-moderate, mostly around the controlled object-state intervention and rollout API. Experiment burden: moderate for the full paper, small for P0. Reviewer vulnerability: high unless event-local causality beats periodic and action-error-triggered controls. Extension potential: three robomimic tasks plus an independent state-based benchmark, without requiring a foundation policy or hardware.

### B. Censoring-aware bounded-action BC — EXCLUDED BEFORE SHORTLIST

**Proposed failure mode and method.** If a logger stores actions after clipping an unbounded command to an action limit, a Gaussian BC target at the limit may be a censored observation rather than the demonstrator’s exact intended command. A censored likelihood could infer the latent command magnitude. The proposed experiment would compare standard BC and censored BC on reach/contact outcomes, after first confirming the data semantics.

**Prior and novelty status.** No exact 2024–2026 robotics result was found. The [AAAI 2025 Truncated Gaussian Policy](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33988) addresses boundary bias in PPO action sampling, not censored demonstration labels. The [2026 Neuro-Inspired Inverse Learning preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24152) addresses Gaussian-BC contraction through forward-model sequence optimization; [Action Quantization BC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20538) studies quantized labels rather than controller clipping. This candidate is not rejected as a novelty collision.

**Admission failure.** The required label-censoring premise is not established for the preferred substrate. In official robosuite v1.5.1, the collector calls the wrapped environment with the action and the DataCollectionWrapper stores that caller action; the HDF5 conversion copies it unchanged. Controller scaling/clipping occurs later. The teleoperation device also clips normalized delta actions to [-1, 1], and robomimic requires actions normalized to that range. See the [v1.5.1 collector](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/v1.5.1/robosuite/scripts/collect_human_demonstrations.py), [v1.5.1 data wrapper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/v1.5.1/robosuite/wrappers/data_collection_wrapper.py), [v1.5.1 device mapping](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/v1.5.1/robosuite/devices/device.py), and [robomimic action convention](https://robomimic.github.io/docs/datasets/overview.html). Some other dataset/configuration might differ, but boundary mass alone would not prove hidden pre-clipped commands. Without a named dataset carrying raw pre-clip and stored post-clip provenance, this is not a viable P0 for the required substrate.

**P0 status.** None on standard robomimic teleoperation data. Reconsider only if a specific public dataset documents pre-clip commands, post-clip stored labels, and sufficient boundary mass. This preserves the novelty reviewer’s PROCEED WITH CAUTION finding while failing the project’s substrate-admission gate.

## 4. Closed-Loop Mechanism Argument

The proposed causal chain is: an external contact or object displacement changes the physical state more than the previous state-action pair predicts; the recurrent hidden state still summarizes the now-invalid pre-event trajectory; clearing that state lets the next action be conditioned on the current state without carrying the stale plan. The falsifiable prediction is not simply lower action error. It is faster recovery and higher task success after a randomized disturbance, specifically when the reset follows a transition residual and beyond what the same number of untimed resets achieves.

There are plausible alternative explanations. A residual may reflect ordinary contact variation, state-estimation noise, or model error rather than a meaningful event. A reset may also erase useful phase information. Therefore the P0 must report nominal false-reset rate, recovery latency, object drops/contact failures, and task success, and must include matched random and periodic reset controls. Residual thresholds are fixed using clean validation rollouts before test disturbances; no threshold tuning on test outcomes is allowed. The mechanism is falsified if event timing has no advantage over count-matched timing or if nominal behavior materially worsens.

## 5. Static Execution Admission

The [robomimic v0.5.0 BC-RNN implementation](https://github.com/ARISE-Initiative/robomimic/blob/v0.5.0/robomimic/algo/bc.py) resets at its configured sequence horizon; that native schedule must remain identical in every arm. The delta can be implemented as a small predictor plus a conditional call to the existing reset operation, without changing policy parameters or adding a learned memory module.

The [robosuite v1.5.1 collector](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/v1.5.1/robosuite/scripts/collect_human_demonstrations.py) and [DataCollectionWrapper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/v1.5.1/robosuite/wrappers/data_collection_wrapper.py) support the source-level distinction needed to reject candidate B: the wrapper records the caller-supplied action while later controller processing may scale or clip it. The [device mapping](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/v1.5.1/robosuite/devices/device.py) also clips normalized delta inputs. The [robomimic data convention](https://robomimic.github.io/docs/datasets/overview.html) expects normalized action labels. Thus the standard preferred stack does not establish that stored boundary actions are censored latent commands.

Candidate B would require a named dataset with verified pre-clip and stored post-clip action provenance and enough boundary observations; that prerequisite is absent. ## 6. Exact 1–3 Day P0 Design

Recommend one P0 only: test transition-residual-triggered recurrent reset on state-only robomimic BC-RNN for Lift and Can.

**Protocol.** Use the existing robomimic v0.5.x and robosuite v1.5.x stack with MuJoCo, proficient-human state-only demonstrations, fixed task splits, and three policy-training seeds per task. Fit a small one-step predictor of the next object and end-effector state from current state and commanded action using only the training split. Freeze its normalization and threshold from a clean validation set before test. For each trained policy, evaluate three inference arms: (1) unchanged BC-RNN with its native horizon reset only; (2) event-triggered full hidden/cell reset when normalized transition residual crosses the frozen threshold; and (3) reset times sampled independently of residual and matched to the event arm’s number of additional resets for each paired rollout. Keep action horizon, policy weights, observations, and all other settings fixed.

**Targeted shift.** On disturbed episodes, after successful grasp and during transport, apply one brief randomized lateral impulse to the object, calibrated in advance to produce approximately 3 cm of lateral displacement within one control interval. Randomize sign and the eligible transport step from a fixed seed schedule. Use the same perturbation schedule for all arms in paired trials. Nominal episodes receive no impulse. This deliberately tests a discrete physical transition surprise rather than visual corruption or language following.

**Rollout budget and metrics.** Run 30 episodes per task × training seed × inference arm × condition (nominal or disturbed), for 1,080 test episodes total. Primary metric is task success. Secondary metrics are return, recovery steps from perturbation to stable progress, object drops, contact failures, trigger recall within two control steps of the impulse, and nominal false-reset episode rate. Action MSE is diagnostic only and cannot satisfy the positive criterion.

**Positive criterion.** In disturbed conditions, the event arm must improve absolute success by at least 10 percentage points over native BC-RNN on both tasks and at least 5 points over count-matched random reset; effect direction must be positive for all three seeds on each task. In nominal conditions, success may not decline by more than 5 points, at least 80% of injected events must trigger within two control steps, and no more than 5% of nominal episodes may receive a false reset. Treat this as a pilot gate, not a final significance claim.

**Kill criteria.** Stop the hypothesis if either task lacks a closed-loop success gain, matched random reset explains the result, only action MSE improves, any seed/task reverses without a supported mechanism, nominal regression exceeds the bound, false resets exceed the bound, or the targeted intervention cannot be applied reproducibly. Also kill if the audit finds a direct prior with the same state-transition-residual-triggered hard reset and recovery claim, or if required behavior cannot be isolated with a small inference wrapper.

**Scope, risk, and budget estimate.** Implementation is limited to the predictor, frozen threshold, one reset hook, one perturbation hook, and metrics. Runtime risk is low-to-moderate: MuJoCo rollouts are lightweight, but the perturbation must preserve reproducible object/task state. Planning estimate: CPU only, up to 8 CPU cores and 24 hours wall-clock, no GPU/DCU, no video, and under 2 GB of incremental artifacts; use existing installed environments and data. These are budget ceilings for a future admitted P0, not resources used in this selection task.

## 7. Planned Full Track-A Experiment Package

If and only if the P0 passes and a separate admission authorizes implementation, the smallest credible RA-L package should add robomimic Square as a third task family, increase seeds, and report paired success/return with confidence intervals. Baselines should include native BC-RNN, event reset, count-matched random reset, periodic reset, an action-error-triggered reset, and a soft hidden-state decay ablation. The core factorial ablations should remove the transition predictor, shift the threshold, vary reset timing, and compare event-local recovery with nominal behavior. Keep policy data, architecture, training updates, rollout horizon, and parameter count matched.

The independent second path is Meta-World ML1 using state-only Pick-Place, Reach, and Drawer-Open policies with a separately collected scripted-expert dataset and the same event/reset comparison. Report task success as primary, with return and event-local recovery diagnostics. This tests whether the mechanism survives a different simulator/task API rather than merely adding more robosuite tasks. The paper should release configuration, seeds, perturbation schedule, and analysis scripts. A positive robomimic P0 alone does not establish cross-benchmark generality.

**Exactly one immediate P0 is recommended:** the 1–3 day Lift + Can state-only BC-RNN test in Section 6. It is a narrow closed-loop falsification of event-conditioned recurrent-state invalidation, uses a mature baseline, has a matched-reset control, and can fail without broad implementation. ## 9. Track-A Versus Track-B Contribution Bar

Candidate A may meet the recalibrated Track-A bar if the P0 supports a reproducible closed-loop effect on both tasks and the event timing beats matched periodic/random resets. Its plausible contribution is an incremental, robot-specific inference mechanism with an isolated causal ablation and a credible independent benchmark path. It is not yet a Track-B claim: novelty is narrow, closest memory-gating work is recent, and broad generality or a new learning paradigm has not been established. Candidate B cannot be advanced under this search because its defining data premise is unsupported on the preferred baseline and its required provenance substrate has not been named.

**Final selection:** retain one cautious hypothesis, transition-surprise-triggered hidden-state reset for BC-RNN; recommend its bounded P0, not execution. Candidate B is excluded before shortlist on substrate grounds. The strongest RA-L reviewer objection is that this is a heuristic reset rule adjacent to learned memory gating, and that a carefully chosen reset schedule could explain any benefit. The event-local, count-matched closed-loop test is therefore the decisive contribution test.
