## Operating frame

The workspace contains the brief and research wiki but no usable experiment harness. The default frame is therefore single-arm or small-morphology tabletop manipulation, public simulation/offline data, standard PyTorch, no real robot, and a pilot that can kill the hypothesis in 1–3 days. A candidate is retained only if its claim is a mechanism or a falsifiable scientific finding. A new wrapper, benchmark score, or generic robustness number is not enough.

## Five initial candidates and aggressive rejection

These five were the initial screen. They are not implementation authorizations.

### A. Active factor-based evaluation of generalist policies — REJECTED

The proposed claim was that sequential experimental design can find failure-prone task-factor regions with fewer evaluations. [Active Real-World Factor-Based Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14439) already makes this exact claim and reports 20–40% fewer real trials. This is a direct prior, not a neighboring idea. It also requires thousands of real evaluations, violating the project’s 1–3 day offline-first constraint.

### B. Adaptive action-chunk horizon for contact-rich manipulation — REJECTED

The proposed claim was that a policy should change its execution horizon at contact or uncertainty boundaries. [Dynamic Execution Horizon Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11408) isolates adaptive execution horizon for black-box chunk policies; [Adaptive Action Chunking](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomimetics11050316) jointly predicts chunk size and actions. A new chunk scheduler would therefore be an incremental architecture/metric change unless it establishes a new causal mechanism. It is also close to the 2026 RACE/action-chunk search results.

### C. Failure-aware counterfactual recovery and correction — REJECTED

The proposed claim was that counterfactual failure rollouts or test-time retries can recover frozen manipulation policies. [Dream2Fix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13528), [CoRe / Imagining Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14822), [FAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01111), [ProbeAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09740), and [LIBERO-RECOVER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05178) already cover synthesized correction, frozen-policy recovery, failure-aware retries, probes/CBF recovery, and recovery evaluation. A different recovery controller would fail the mandatory-anchor and 2026-neighbor test.

### D. Contact-topology transfer boundary for cross-embodiment policies — SURVIVES WITH CAUTION

This is not “another cross-embodiment representation.” The claim is that transfer error has a phase boundary caused by a mismatch in the *contact-mode graph* available to the source and target embodiments, even when visual appearance, end-effector traces, and gross kinematics are matched. The candidate tests when existing transfer interfaces cease to preserve the action consequences needed for contact-rich manipulation.

### E. Common-random intervention coupling for world-model policy comparison — SURVIVES WITH CAUTION

This is not “world-model evaluation under distribution shift.” The claim is that paired policy comparisons using identical start states and shared exogenous generation noise reduce ranking variance and false policy-selection flips at a fixed rollout budget. It changes the estimator and experimental mechanism, not the world model or its uncertainty head. It remains risky because the public WorldGym harness is heavyweight and the contribution could be judged as an evaluation protocol.

## Retained candidate 1: Contact-topology transfer boundary

### Scientific hypothesis

For cross-embodiment manipulation, transfer quality is controlled more by preservation of task-relevant contact modes (approach, establish, maintain, slide/regrasp, release) than by morphology distance or visual similarity. When the target embodiment cannot realize a source contact-mode transition, behavior-aligned or kinematic interfaces should fail systematically even if the task image and end-effector trace look aligned.

### Exact non-overlapping claim

Existing work shows that alignment can improve average transfer. This candidate claims a *mechanistic failure boundary*: a measurable contact-topology mismatch predicts transfer failure and explains residual errors that geometry- or representation-based transfer cannot explain. A negative result—no topology effect after strict matching—would also kill the hypothesis cleanly.

### Closest-prior-work table

| Prior work | Exact prior claim | What it already solves | Candidate’s non-overlapping claim | Distinguishing experiment | Difference type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [CEI, RA-L 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09163) | A 3D cross-embodiment interface uses functional similarity and trajectory alignment to transfer across 16 simulated embodiments and real grippers. | Broad morphology/trajectory transfer. | Functional similarity is insufficient when the source and target have different realizable contact-mode transitions. | Hold object/task/camera and gross EEF trajectory fixed; sweep only gripper/contact topology and compare transfer error, contact-mode completion, and recovery. | **Scientific mechanism**, not a new benchmark score. |
| [Behavior-Aligned Representations, ICRA 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27549) | EEF traces, language motions, and boxes improve cross-embodiment VLA transfer and can use action-free data. | Representation-based transfer and action-free-data benefit. | EEF/visual alignment can hide contact-mode mismatch; the mismatch predicts where gains disappear. | Matched source/target pairs with equal representation quality but different contact graphs; test whether topology predicts failures. | **Scientific mechanism**. |
| [Transferring Contact, Not Just Motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15516) | A calibrated force-position interface transfers compliant grasping across heterogeneous dexterous hands. | Contact-aware transfer with force sensing and calibration. | A low-cost, offline diagnostic can identify topology-induced transfer limits before adding tactile hardware. | Compare kinematic-only, contact-mode, and force-aware predictors on simulated grasp/contact transitions; no claim to replace force control. | **Scientific mechanism**, with a narrower observation regime. |
| [Cloak, CoRL 2026](https://tml.stanford.edu/cloak/) | Masking the end-effector from the wrist view enables zero-shot visual cross-embodiment transfer. | Removes embodiment-specific visual shortcut. | Visual invariance does not imply action-affordance invariance at contact transitions. | Pair Cloak-style visual masking with controlled contact-topology swaps and test whether failures persist after visual cues are removed. | **Scientific mechanism**. |
| Embodiment scaling laws ([Ai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05753)) | More training embodiments improve locomotion generalization, including topological and kinematic variation. | Scaling evidence for embodiment diversity, mainly locomotion. | Manipulation transfer has a contact-topology threshold distinct from aggregate embodiment count. | Same number of source embodiments, vary contact graph versus geometric/joint variation, and compare generalization. | **Scientific mechanism**, different task family. |

The mandatory data-selection anchors (S2I, SCIZOR, DataMIL) do not solve contact-topology transfer; they act on which data are selected. LIBERO-CF/VIFO/Safety do not solve it; they study language, visual cues, and safety. WorldEval/WorldGym/IRASim/WorldEcho/decision-centric evaluation do not solve it; they evaluate learned simulators or policy behavior rather than the embodiment transfer mechanism. The candidate must state these separations explicitly in any later paper.

### Minimum falsifiable pilot

1. Use an existing public simulation benchmark and robot models with at least two gripper/contact topologies; do not build a new simulator.
2. Select a frozen source policy or public transfer baseline (CEI/behavior-aligned representation where runnable).
3. Create matched pairs varying one factor at a time: visual appearance, EEF geometry, joint kinematics, and contact-mode graph.
4. Measure task success, contact-mode completion, first irreversible failure step, and transfer error at equal observation/action budgets.
5. Fit a preregistered simple predictor using contact-mode mismatch and compare it with morphology distance and visual embedding distance.

**Positive signal**: contact-mode mismatch predicts failure after controlling for visual and geometric distance, across at least two contact-rich task families.

**Kill signal**: topology adds no predictive value, or the result requires a new contact sensor/simulator unavailable in the public setup.

### Feasibility

- Pilot: simulation/offline analysis; no hardware required.
- Baselines: direct transfer, CEI-style alignment, behavior-aligned representation, and visual-masking control.
- Compute: low to medium; small policy inference and tabular/statistical analysis should fit standard PyTorch/DCU use.
- Implementation effort: roughly 1–3 days only if an existing benchmark/checkpoint runs; otherwise stop rather than repair infrastructure.
- Runtime risk: medium. CEI/BAR-X artifact availability and simulator compatibility must be verified before authorization.
- Reviewer rejection: “This is a benchmark stratification or a post-hoc metric.” The result must show a controlled causal factor and predictive failure boundary, not just a new score.
- Publication potential: moderate-to-good CoRL/ICRA/RA-L if the boundary is reproducible and leads to a practical transfer-selection rule; weak if it remains a single benchmark analysis.
- Longer-term extension: contact-aware cross-embodiment interfaces, morphology curriculum design, and sim-to-real transfer diagnostics.

## Retained candidate 2: Common-random intervention coupling for world-model policy comparison

### Scientific hypothesis

When an action-conditioned world model is used to compare two policies, independent Monte Carlo rollouts inject avoidable exogenous video-generation variance into the policy difference. Pairing both policies on the same initial state and shared exogenous generation/noise branches should reduce the variance of the *difference estimator*, lower false rank flips, and reach a correct selection with fewer rollouts than independent evaluation.

### Exact non-overlapping claim

WorldEval and WorldGym establish correlation and ranking preservation; WorldEcho establishes off-expert action-following tests; the decision-centric position asks for ranking and uncertainty evidence. This candidate does not claim a better world model, better shift robustness, or generic uncertainty calibration. It claims a variance-reduction mechanism for paired policy comparisons under a fixed world-model evaluator.

### Closest-prior-work table

| Prior work | Exact prior claim | What it already solves | Candidate’s non-overlapping claim | Distinguishing experiment | Difference type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [WorldGym, ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/7f5e909ac0324db03506b380c695ffaf-Abstract-Conference.html) | Monte Carlo rollouts in a world model correlate with real success and preserve relative policy rankings. | Provides a learned evaluation environment and ranking evidence. | Shared stochastic branches can make pairwise ranking more statistically efficient and stable. | Compare independent versus common-random-number paired rollouts at equal total samples; report rank-flip probability and Kendall agreement with held-out real/simulator labels. | **Scientific estimator mechanism**, not distribution-shift evaluation. |
| [WorldEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19017) | Policy2Vec enables policy video generation, ranking/checkpoint evaluation, and hazard detection with real-world correlation. | Policy evaluation and hazard screening. | Difference-of-outcomes estimation, rather than absolute policy scoring, benefits from coupling. | Same policy pairs, same initial scenes, paired latent branches; compare confidence intervals and decisions. | **Scientific estimator mechanism**. |
| [IRASim, ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zhu_IRASim_A_Fine-Grained_World_Model_for_Robot_Manipulation_ICCV2025_paper.pdf) | Fine-grained action-conditioned video can support policy evaluation and planning. | High-fidelity action-conditioned generation and planning use case. | Coupling is orthogonal to model architecture and tests whether its stochasticity can be canceled in comparisons. | Reuse released evaluator outputs/checkpoint if possible; only the sampling protocol changes. | **Scientific estimator mechanism**. |
| [WorldEcho / WorldSync](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24885) | Tests and improves off-expert action following using coverage, grounding, and intervention alignment. | Action-fidelity diagnosis and model alignment. | Even an action-faithful model can make noisy pairwise policy decisions; coupling targets comparison variance, not action following. | Evaluate the same paired policies on expert and non-expert branches while holding branch noise fixed across policies. | **Scientific estimator mechanism**. |
| [Decision-centric position](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15032) | Recommends policy-ranking agreement, optimization lift, exploitability, and uncertainty under an explicit decision contract. | A protocol for what evidence a world-model claim should report. | Supplies one concrete estimator-level intervention and a budgeted test of ranking reliability. | Add paired-vs-independent comparison to the proposed protocol and test whether decision errors fall at fixed rollout budget. | **Mechanism**, but publication risk is high if only a protocol improvement is shown. |

The remaining mandatory anchors are screened as follows: S2I/SCIZOR/DataMIL concern demonstration selection, LIBERO-CF/VIFO/Safety concern language/visual cues/safety, and none supplies a paired world-model estimator. This candidate must not drift into their banned themes.

### Minimum falsifiable pilot

1. Reuse WorldGym’s public evaluator only if its checkpoint, code, and controllable random state are available without rebuilding the LIBERO runtime. The official repository advertises a roughly 9 GB checkpoint and JAX/TensorFlow dependencies; this is a feasibility gate, not an installation request.
2. Select a small set of frozen policy pairs and held-out initial frames with public outcome labels.
3. Run independent Monte Carlo and paired common-random branches with equal total rollout count.
4. Measure estimator variance, confidence-interval coverage, Kendall/Spearman agreement, false rank flips, and rollouts per correct selection.

**Positive signal**: paired evaluation reduces rank-flip probability or required rollouts at matched accuracy across multiple policy pairs and does not worsen calibration.

**Kill signal**: the world-model interface does not expose shareable stochastic branches, coupling produces no reduction, or the result is only a re-labeling of uncertainty intervals.

### Feasibility

- Pilot: offline/analysis-first if released rollouts are sufficient; otherwise small simulation evaluation.
- Compute: medium to high because the public WorldGym stack is heavy; DCU compatibility is unverified and must not be assumed.
- Implementation effort: 1–3 days only if the evaluator already runs and random branches can be controlled; otherwise stop.
- Runtime risk: high relative to candidate 1.
- Reviewer rejection: “Common random numbers are a generic statistical trick, not a robotics contribution.” The paper would need a robotics-specific policy-selection failure analysis and cross-task evidence.
- Publication potential: modest unless the reduction is large and changes deployment decisions; likely a methods/evaluation paper rather than a central policy-learning contribution.
- Longer-term extension: budgeted policy selection, paired world-model planning, and evaluator decision contracts.

## Hard eliminations retained from the search

- Segment/state-action demonstration filtering: already covered by S2I, SCIZOR, and DataMIL.
- Generic mixed-quality data selection: same anchors; no new candidate.
- Visual shortcut/counterfactual language diagnosis: LIBERO-CF and CAG already establish and mitigate it.
- Generic VLA safety benchmarking: LIBERO-VIFO and LIBERO-Safety already cover visual-cue and physical/semantic safety.
- World-model degradation under shift: explicitly banned and subsumed by WorldGym/WorldEval/WorldEcho/decision-centric evaluation.
- Generic world-model uncertainty calibration: explicitly covered by the decision-centric position and adjacent benchmarks.
- Generic off-expert action-following diagnosis: explicitly covered by WorldEcho/WorldSync.
- Active factor-based policy evaluation: exact 2026 prior [Active Real-World Factor-Based Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14439).
- Generic recovery/retry: Dream2Fix, CoRe, FAR, ProbeAct, and LIBERO-RECOVER.
- Generic continual forgetting: existing continual robot-learning benchmarks and 2026 continual VLA/manipulation work make a simple forgetting study insufficient.

## Revised ranking and stop decision

1. **Contact-topology transfer boundary** — retained; strongest fit to the brief, but only after verifying an existing public benchmark/checkpoint path.
2. **Common-random intervention coupling for world-model policy comparison** — retained provisionally; scientifically distinct but high runtime and publication risk.

No third candidate clears the requested novelty and execution gates. The first-pass Top-3 remain unauthorized. This report stops here as requested; it does not authorize implementation, runtime installation, checkpoint download, or experiments.
